## 8. What is reconciliation, once the models are portable?

Part 1 ended on a claim it is now time to cash out: reconciliation is not the point of a lift but one
**use** of the portable model a lift produces — the flagship use, and the hardest, but a use. Seeing it
that way changes what the rest of this report is doing. It is no longer cataloguing a bespoke procedure;
it is studying what happens when the portable models of Part 1 are put to their most demanding job.

Reconciliation aligns two lifted models: it works out which concept on one side denotes the same thing,
or stands in a definite relation to, which concept on the other; fixes the shared attributes so they
cannot be misread; aligns the individuals; and confirms the result. Structurally it is a **dyadic
operation** with a specific shape. Each system is at once a **consumer** of the other's model — reading
it exactly as the independent consumer of Part 1 read a single lift — and the **authority** over its
own, the only party entitled to ratify what the other proposes about its concepts. A correspondence
closes only when the owner accepts it: by **declaration** (the owner says "yes, that is what I mean") or
by **evidence** (a decisive virtual experiment — provision the candidate through the proposed
correspondence, operate it, read it back, check the invariants).

That decomposition is what ties Part 2 back to Part 1, and it is worth stating plainly:

> **The consumption underneath reconciliation is the same monadic portability measured in Part 1,
> applied twice and coupled by authority. Reconciliation is a dyadic operation built from two monadic
> reads.**

So the difficulty of reconciliation is not a new phenomenon; it is a diagnostic of an old one.

> **Reconciliation is hard exactly to the degree the lifts fell short of full portability. The
> machinery it needs — a shared reference, construct-then-bind, active verification, authority
> ratification — is portability repair, carried out interactively between two systems.**

Two *fully* portable models reconcile almost trivially: each consumes the other, finds the shared ground
already present in the self-describing concepts, and the binding falls out with nothing to negotiate.
Difficulty appears precisely where the lifts are meaning-poor, locally anchored, or unbridged — the same
shortfall a reference-in-the-lift repaired on the production side in Part 1, now repaired across the
seam. This reframing does not diminish the reconciliation results that follow; it *organises* them. Each
setting becomes a measurement of repair under a particular kind of shortfall, and the one thing repair
never reaches — the authority to decide *whose* value governs — is the governance residue Part 1's
definition already carved off, reappearing here as the irreducible human or authoritative act at the end
of an otherwise autonomous process.

## 9. Where do models diverge, and what does each kind of divergence need?

Reconciliation is not one problem. The temptation is to meet that variety with a list of tricks; the
better move is a **map**, because the ways two models can diverge fall along two principled axes that
tie directly to the lifted-model architecture of Part 1 — and, laid out, the map does three jobs at
once: it **predicts** the repair each kind of divergence needs, it **places** the study's settings as
samples of a space rather than a grab-bag, and it doubles as a **coverage map** that shows what has been
tested and what has not.

**The first axis: identity ↔ relation.** At one end, the two models hold the *same thing in different
representations*, and a correspondence is an **identity** — this term simply *is* that term. At the
other end, the two models hold *different but related things* that meet at a seam, and a correspondence
is a **relation**: one concept *realises*, *governs*, *abstracts*, or *intends* another. This axis
matters because it sets what "getting it right" even means. An identity can be checked by round-trip; a
relation cannot, and must be confirmed against the specific relation that holds (a bound is *satisfied*,
not equalled; an underlay is *ridden*, not renamed). The study's easy corner is pure identity; its hard
cases all live at the relation end.

**The second axis: the layer at which the divergence sits.** The lifted model has layers, and two models
can agree at one and diverge at another. Each layer, when it is where the divergence lives, calls for a
different repair — and this is the axis that turns the map into a prediction:

- **Schematic** (vocabulary and structure — concepts, kinds, relations). Repair: a **shared-category
  reference**, taken off the shelf where a standard exists or **constructed** where none does.
- **Instance** (which live individual is which). Repair: **entity resolution** over keys, attributes, and
  topology, sometimes settled only by **probing the live system**.
- **Provenance** (which authority is the source of record for a shared value). Repair: **authority
  ratification** — a declaration, or a derivation from an interconnect artefact.
- **Pragmatic** (what a reconciled thing is *for*, whether a trade-off is acceptable, whether a signal
  *matters*). Repair: **live elicitation** or a **pre-placed policy** that carries a party's judgement to
  where it is needed — and the one layer no reference can reach, because what is missing is judgement,
  not information.
- **Abstraction / layer** (the same situation described at different layers of a stack). Repair: a
  **structural dependency map** across the layers, along which separately reconciled parts compose.

The payoff of drawing it this way is that the study's four settings stop looking like four topics and
start looking like a deliberate traverse of the space:

| Setting | Identity ↔ relation | Layer of divergence | Repair the case exercises |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Configuration** (TAPI ↔ TEAS) | identity (equivalence) | schematic | shared reference substitutes for cognition; instance co-reference |
| **2 · Intent** (intent ↔ realisation) | relation (refinement / satisfaction) | pragmatic | pre-placed movable policy; the limit at authority |
| **3 · Cross-domain** (Meridian ↔ Cascade) | relation (underlay) | schematic + provenance | **constructed** reference (no standard); authority attribution |
| **4 · Observability** (alarm ↔ anomaly) | relation (ontological; one-to-many) | instance + pragmatic + abstraction | reference pins meaning; pragmatics carry significance; dependency map composes |

Two folded studies extend the reach past the settings' headline corners. Authority derivation and
conflict (§13.x) exercise the **provenance** layer directly — deriving which realm is the source of
record for a contested field from an interconnect artefact, and deferring when it is genuinely co-owned —
which is why pure provenance-divergence, easy to mistake for a gap in the map, is in fact sampled. And
active verification (§13.7) is the worked case of the **relation/schematic corner under repair by
evidence**: where a constructed correspondence is a plausible cognate that description alone cannot
settle, the decisive virtual experiment closes it.

Read as a coverage map, the traverse is broad: both ends of the identity–relation axis, and every layer
of divergence, appear at least once, with the hard relation-end cases carrying the weight. The honest
edges are the ones the map makes visible rather than hides — the pure abstraction/layer corner is
touched only through observability's correlation task, and the pragmatic layer's authority residue is,
by construction, the place the whole programme stops and hands to a person.

> **Every reconciliation type is two portable models meeting under a relationship; reconciliation is
> portability repair at the layer where they diverge. That single sentence is what makes Part 2 one
> argument rather than four case studies — and it is why Part 2 stands on Part 1.**

The rest of Part 2 works down from this map. It first names the **operations** a reconciliation is built
from (the repair steps, made concrete), then takes each setting in turn as a measured sample of the
space — configuration and the identity corner, intent and the pragmatic layer, cross-domain and the
constructed reference, observability and significance — and closes with the synthesis: what works, how
far, and exactly where the automation hands off.
