# Deck design brief — the "reel them in" deck (governing doc)

Working brief for the presentation deck. This REPLACES the earlier plan (deck as a mirror of the master
report, which had grown to dozens of slides). Build the deck against this brief, not the report's structure.

## Purpose and audience (what the deck is FOR)

The deck is **top-of-funnel**. Its reader has NOT committed — they are "testing the waters," skimming to
answer one question: *is this worth my attention?* They will decide in ~2 minutes. So:
- The deck's success metric is **conversion** (does it make them want the report/paper), NOT comprehension
  of every result.
- It is a **lure/teaser**, a different GENRE from the report. The report is for someone already in the boat
  and wants the evidence (depth, rigor, completeness). The deck is what decides whether they get in the boat.
- Mirroring the report in the deck is a CATEGORY ERROR — it dumps the full argument on someone who hasn't
  decided they care, and loses them by slide six of forty.

## Length and the selection test

Up to **~15 slides — a CEILING, not a target.** Every slide must earn its place by the **reel-in test**:
*does it hook or deepen interest?* — NOT "is this an important result?" (that's the report's test). Most of
our results FAIL the reel-in test — not because they're weak, but because they're load-bearing for *rigor*
(report's job), not *desire* (deck's job). Kill the darlings ruthlessly. The headroom to 15 is for BREATHING
ROOM (let a strong finding land on its own slide) and one or two QUESTION interstitials — NOT for a 4th/5th
finding. Three well-chosen lures per part beats five.

## The reel-in mechanic

**Question-driven.** Each question is an OPEN LOOP; each key finding CLOSES one loop and OPENS the next
(question → glimpse of an answer → bigger question). That cadence IS the reel, built into the spine, and it
licenses selectivity: a question needs only ONE finding to answer it enough to move on.

## The arc (slide-by-slide skeleton — fill numbers from the runs)

1. **HOOK — the startling question**, feet on the floor: *do two sufficiently cognitive systems need
   pre-defined data models at all to communicate for a purpose, or can they come up with a suitable one on
   their own?* Forecast: the answer is shown to be YES (with nuance). Carry a one-line acknowledgment of the
   reality on the SAME slide (decades of legacy inert systems, data of mixed quality, high-reliability
   systems that must work in all circumstances) so the provocation lands as INFORMED, not wide-eyed.
2. **THE BURDEN** — the never-ending standard-model quest; the pain the audience feels. Could cognition
   lighten it?
3. **THE REFRAME** — machine cognition changes the premise. Introduce the **ad hoc semantic model** and
   **portability** (with its one-line operational definition: a sufficiently-reasoning agent can make
   arbitrary use of the package without seeking clarification outside it).
4. **THE PATH QUESTIONS** — what do the mechanics need to look like? how do we break it down and investigate?
   how do we get TRACTABLY from A to B? (Transition is a first-class character, not a footnote.)
5–7. **PART 1 — portability: three curated lures** (see below). Questions + chosen key findings that hint or
   show solid conclusions.
8. **THE RECONCILIATION TURN** — why reconciliation is the important "use case" for ad hoc semantic models.
9–10. **PART 2 — reconciliation: two lures** (see below).
11. **RECAP — the promise held.**
12. **TRANSFORMATIVE CLOSE** — this is big; it could change a lot, INCLUDING what we attack in standards
    (redirection, not elimination). The invitation to the full work.
(~12 slides; up to 15 with breathing room / question interstitials if the flow earns it.)

## The lure findings (candidates — pick the sharpest; fill exact numbers from the runs)

Selection = "makes a skimmer lean in / believe the promise," not "we're proud of it."
- **P1 (portability baseline):** a well-lifted model is comprehensible to strong, middle, AND even weak
  reasoners from the package alone (E22 Phase 1–2). The "cognitive systems can just get each other's models"
  proof; legible in one breath.
- **P2 (cognition builds the ground):** where the source is too thin to understand, cognition BUILDS the
  missing common ground itself (construct-then-bind / reference-in-lift). The direct answer to the burden
  question — they don't need a standard handed to them; they can make one.
- **R1 (reconciliation without a standard, guarded):** two systems that never agreed on anything bind their
  models correctly AND REFUSE the wrong bindings (block false cognates), at the cost of a SHORT GLOSSARY, not
  a standard (E21, cognate-guarded). The refusal is the credibility clincher (disciplined, not naïve
  agreement); the "cost is a glossary" line lands the standards-burden payoff.
- **Honest guardrail (keep visible):** the pure-authority/governance residue is NOT dissolved — a human/
  authoritative act remains where authority, not meaning, is at stake.

## GOVERNING TONE — feet on the floor AND audacious (hold at EVERY point)

The balance is not managed *despite* the bold question — the rigor is what EARNS it. Put the hard realities
INSIDE the frame, up front; don't defend against them slide by slide.
- **Audacious in the questions and the possibility; measured in the evidence; actionable in the implication.**
- **Plain words, no jargon.** The deck has no room to define terms, so it uses none that need defining.
  Uncommon/technical words like **monadic** and **dyadic** are BANNED from the deck (they belong in the
  report, where they are defined at first use). Say "a model that stands on its own" and "two systems
  meeting", not "monadic" and "dyadic". If a word would make a smart non-specialist reach for a definition,
  it fails the reel-in test — cut it.
- **Evolutionary, not revolutionary.** The provocation is "do they need PRE-AGREED models?", not "models are
  over." The forecast answer: production and agreement can increasingly be AD HOC, lifting the pre-agreement
  burden — coexisting with legacy, not razing it.
- **Our own rigor IS the feet-on-floor / the persuasion.** We tested thin, meaning-poor lifts (data quality
  varies); cognition is capability-banded and the weak tier confabulates (no magic); reconciliation REFUSES
  wrong bindings (reliability is the bar); the residue needs authority (we know where the human stays). Each
  limit doubles as a credibility signal.
- **Transition (A→B) is first-class.** Frame the work as MAPPING THE PATH — inert legacy and cognitive
  systems coexisting, mixed-quality lifts handled gracefully, the reference/anchoring the tool that makes
  each step SAFE. That turns "transformative" from fantasy into roadmap.
- **Transformative = redirection, not elimination.** Claim we can change WHAT WE ATTACK in standards and
  LIGHTEN the burden — strategic and actionable — never "standards are obsolete / you're wasting your time".
- **The five naïve traps to never trip:** cognition-overnight; reliability-hand-waved; no-discipline-at-all;
  transition-ignored; weak-tier-oversold. If a slide trips one, it's wrong however good the hook sounds.

This tone holds not just at the opening but at EVERY point — and it governs the whole rewrite too, the deck
just being where the tension (boldest hook) is sharpest.

## Relationship to the report/paper
Same portability spine (Part 1 lift/portability, Part 2 reconciliation-as-use), but told as a STORY that
hooks, not a summary that mirrors. Deck = the lure; report/paper = the evidence for the committed reader.
