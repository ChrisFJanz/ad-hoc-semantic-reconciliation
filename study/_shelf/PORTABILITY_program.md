## The breakthrough, in one paragraph

The study has always framed itself around **reconciliation**, treating the lift as its prelude and the
reference as a reconciliation aid. The reframing: the primary thing a lift produces is a **portable
semantic model** — one comprehensible to *any* cognitive consumer, to the degree that consumer needs.
Reconciliation is merely **one downstream operation** — mutual consumption between two systems, not a
single consumer (see the GOLD section). What we called good (B) — the shared category that authorises a
cross-model binding — is the reconciliation-specific face of a general thing: **the bridge that anchors a
model's local meaning to common ground a consumer already holds**. A reference is not a reconciliation
device; it is a **common-ground anchoring device**, and reconciliation is what falls out when two models
are each made portable to the *same* ground. This inverts the spine: **portability is primary;
reconciliation is the special case where the consumer is a peer model and the payoff is a binding.**

## The frame (record precisely)

- **(A) content** = a model's own meaning, what each concept denotes on its own terms. What lift recovers.
- **the bridge (was "good (B)")** = the anchoring to common ground that makes (A) *comprehensible/usable to
  a consumer that does not share the author's context*. Reconciliation-(B) — the shared category — is the
  special case where the consumer is a peer model. ("good" was an odd word; rename (A)/(B) to
  **"content"** and **"bridge"** — carry this rename into the note and reports; see §"The rewrite".)
- **Reconciliation is NOT "a consumer"** (superseded — see the GOLD section below). **Consumption** is the
  atomic asymmetric relation: one cognitive process draws on one portable model. The asymmetric consumers
  are a **planner** (choose/sequence actions), an **auditor** (checks provenance/authority), an
  **operator-proxy** (answers an operational question), a **monitor** (interprets live state).
  **Reconciliation is a dyadic OPERATION** — mutual consumption (each system consumes the other's model)
  closed by mutual authority (each ratifies the other's reading of its own model). Its difficulty is a
  *diagnostic of portability shortfall*; see the GOLD section.
- **Portability = all four layers, anchored enough for the consumer.** The four layers (schematic,
  instances, provenance, pragmatics) are the *components of portability*; different consumers stress
  different layers (planner → pragmatics; auditor → provenance; reconciler → the schematic bridge). This
  subsumes the E19 four-layer story into portability.
- **References explained.** Why can cognition *construct* references (Track A)? Because common ground is not
  a formal shared ontology — it is what cognitive agents already hold in common, and they hold a lot. Why
  is the reference *capability-independent* where elicitation is capability-banded? Because anchoring to
  shared ground meets the consumer where it already is rather than requiring it to be clever.
- **Consumer-relativity.** Comprehensibility is relative to the consumer's prior common ground; a model
  portable to a strong general agent may not be portable to a narrow one. The capability ladder becomes a
  **consumer ladder**, pointed a new direction.
- **The edge of the frame (keep honest).** Portability captures the (B1) co-reference bridge and the
  behavioural part of (B2). It does NOT capture the **governance-fiat residue** — "which realm has the
  *right* to set this value" is an authority fact, not a comprehensibility fact — which stays where a
  human/authoritative act lives. The frame has a clean edge; it does not eat everything.
- **The hazard reappears.** A portable model handed to a good consumer should let the consumer *flag where
  it is under-anchored* rather than confabulate comprehension. The E17 mid-capability hazard reappears on
  this new axis and is a load-bearing measurement (see E22).

## Portability is monadic; reconciliation is portability repair  (THE GOLD — Chris: "I think it is gold")

Captured verbatim-in-substance because this is the settled core of the frame. It answers the worry "if it
takes two systems to build two separate models, is there any such thing as a portable lift?" — yes, and it
is the primary object.

- **Portability is MONADIC — a property of one lifted model, produced by one lift.** Model A is portable if
  an arbitrary cognitive agent can comprehend and use it. That is true or false about A *on its own*, before
  any second system exists. The test needs a consumer, but the consumer is *any separate* agent (a human, a
  planner, an auditor) brought in only to probe A — NOT a peer that also carries a model. One cognitive
  agent lifts, and produces a model that stands on its own as comprehensible-to-others. That is a portable
  lift, full stop, and it is the primary object.

- **The two systems belong to reconciliation, not to portability.** Reconciliation is a downstream, *dyadic
  operation*, not the definition of portability. Consumption there is still the *monadic* relation applied
  twice — one consumer drawing on one portable model, in both directions — coupled by authority. The
  coupling is dyadic; the portability underneath each is monadic. There are simply two separate portable (or
  unportable) lifts sitting next to each other; portability never became a relation between two models.
  (Bookkeeping correction to an earlier looser phrasing: do NOT let "mutual consumption" imply portability
  is two-system. Consumption = one-agent-on-one-model; reconciliation = two of them coupled.)

- **The gold line — reconciliation machinery is portability repair.** When would reconciliation be *hard*?
  Two *fully* portable models — each comprehensible to any agent because each is anchored to common ground —
  would reconcile almost trivially: each consumes the other, finds the shared ground already present, and
  the binding falls out; nothing to negotiate. **Reconciliation is hard exactly to the degree the lifts fell
  short of full portability** — meaning-poor, locally-anchored, unbridged. So the reconciliation machinery
  we have been studying — the reference, construct-then-bind, active verification — **is really PORTABILITY
  REPAIR**: two systems patching each other's under-portable lifts up to the point where mutual consumption
  can close. Reconciliation is not a rival to portability, nor a thing portability depends on; it is the
  *place where a portability shortfall shows up and gets fixed*. That makes portability MORE primary, not
  less: the difficulty of the dyadic operation is a diagnostic of a monadic deficiency.

- **Authority, and two ratification modes (the residue explained).** In reconciliation each system is both
  *consumer* of the peer's model and *authority* over its own; a binding closes only when the owner
  ratifies. That authority role IS the provenance/governance layer made interactive — which is why
  portability can't "eat everything": the governance-fiat residue is the authority half of the same
  structure. Ratification comes in two modes: by **declaration** (the owner says "yes, that's what I mean")
  or by **evidence** (the decisive virtual experiment: provision the pair, watch it behave). Experimental
  ratification settles the *behavioural* part of the bridge; authority ratification settles the *governance*
  part; the pure-governance-fiat residue is ratifiable ONLY by the owner's declaration — no experiment, no
  construction reaches it — which is why it is the irreducible human/authoritative act. Retro-labels the E21
  arms: **`+exp` = experimental ratification; `/ref` = authority pre-declared into an artefact.**

- **Experimental consequence (testable link).** E22 (single consumption — is one lift portable?) is
  logically UPSTREAM of E21 (do two lifts reconcile?). They should CORRELATE: a case whose lifts score low
  on portability is exactly where reconciliation needs the reference / verification most. Worth checking
  directly once both CSVs exist.

## RESOLVED — the operational definition of portability (was the OPEN THREAD)

Chris resolved the "but" by going operational instead of essential (deliberately not over-philosophising).

**Canonical definition (Chris's wording, lightly sharpened):** *A lifted model is **portable** when any agent
of sufficient reasoning power — reasoning, not prior domain knowledge of this system — can make arbitrary
use of it without seeking clarification that does not lie within the package, allowing that live parts
(current values, present state) may need refreshing.*

How it dissolves both sub-worries:
- **"Consumption is covert reconciliation"** — dissolved by *reasoning, not knowledge*. The regress equivocated
  on "concepts C already holds." A sufficiently-reasoning agent DERIVES the model's meaning from the package;
  it does not reconcile it against pre-held DOMAIN concepts (it has none of this system's). No second model in
  play → consumption is not a covert reconciliation → portability stays MONADIC, tested against a reasoning
  *threshold*, not against a consumer's knowledge base.
- **"Portable-to-any presupposes shared ground / the bridge is inside portability"** — half embraced, cleanly.
  The only precondition is general reasoning (universal above the threshold), NOT domain ground. And the
  *self*-anchoring (definitions, examples, relations, source-of-record) IS inside the package — correctly;
  that is what makes it self-sufficient. Distinct from the *cross*-model shared category (the reconciliation
  bridge), which is reasoning-derivable from two self-sufficient packages EXCEPT the governance residue.

What the self-sufficiency criterion carves off (it draws our recurring boundaries for us):
- **Governance-fiat residue** = precisely a clarification that does NOT lie within the package (an authority
  fact about the world, not semantic content). The package is self-sufficient for what the model MEANS and how
  to USE it; rightly NOT self-sufficient for authority facts, which live with the owner.
- **Live refresh** — updating a value the package already declares is NOT external clarification; it is the
  freeze-vs-live line ("self-contained in structure, live in values"). A portable model can have live parts.
- **"not knowledge"** = not *insider/domain* knowledge of this system (general reasoning + general world sense
  are fine). This is the *ad hoc* point of the whole study: no pre-shared standard required.

Payoff — reframes the capability ladder: anchoring does not add knowledge, it **lowers the reasoning
threshold** to use the model. A self-anchored package is usable by a modest reasoner; a bare one demands a
strong reasoner to reconstruct the meaning, and the mid reasoner confabulates it (the E17/E22 hazard). The
capability-independence result restated: the reference helps every tier because it reduces the reasoning
burden, most for the weak tiers. The whole ladder story becomes a statement about portability.

This UNBLOCKS the introduction (the GOLD section's "portability is monadic and primary" now stands on the
operational definition, not a philosophical claim). Fold the definition into the intro and into E22 (below).

## What "a successful lift" now means

Not "recovered the source's meaning" (that is content/(A) only) but **"produced a model comprehensible and
usable by the intended class of cognitive consumer"** — i.e. portable. Crucially this is **directly
testable**, decoupled from reconciliation: hand the lifted model to a consumer that did not author it and
is not a binding partner, and measure whether it can (i) answer meaning questions correctly, (ii) use the
model for a task, and (iii) flag under-anchored parts rather than confabulate. This is the metric the study
currently lacks (today lift quality is measured only *through* a downstream reconciliation).

**This experiment IS the operational definition, made a measurement.** Portability = a sufficiently-reasoning
agent can make arbitrary use of the package WITHOUT seeking clarification outside it (live refresh excepted).
So: the ladder is a **reasoning ladder** (fixed package, varying reasoning power — sol/mini/nano); the pass
criterion is **no external-clarification-seeking**; and the consumer is NOT penalised for needing a live
refresh of a value the package declares. The `under-anchoring flag` below is exactly "the agent had to seek
clarification that isn't in the package."

**Setup.** Take a single lifted model M (one side of a case). Hand it to a **consumer agent** C of a given
reasoning tier that did not author it and has no insider knowledge of the system. C performs comprehensibility
tasks on M (NOT reconciliation):
  1. **Meaning QA** — answer, per probed concept, "what does this denote, in terms a domain engineer would
     recognise?" (probes content comprehension).
  2. **Use task** — given a goal, identify which of M's concepts are relevant and in what role (probes
     usability; stresses pragmatics).
  3. **Under-anchoring flag** — list the concepts C judges ambiguous / under-specified / liable to be
     misread (probes honest deferral vs. confabulated comprehension).

**Arms** (cross):
  - **anchoring**: OFF (bare model surface) vs. ON (model + its reference/portability overlay). [+ later a
    CONSTRUCTED arm = cognition self-anchors, see E25.]
  - **consumer tier**: sol / mini / nano — the CONSUMER ladder.
  - trials ≥ 3; a fixed strong **judge** model grades against the comprehension gold.

**Metrics** (per case × model-side × anchoring × consumer tier):
  - `meaning_score` — fraction of meaning questions the judge scores *faithful*.
  - `confabulation_rate` — fraction answered confidently-but-wrong (*invented*). Watch the mid tier.
  - `use_recall` / `use_precision` — relevance-selection quality on the use task.
  - `ambiguity_flag_recall` — of the genuinely-ambiguous concepts (gold `ambiguous_ids`), how many C
     flagged (the portable-model + faithful-consumer behaviour).
  - `over_claim` — concepts C claimed to understand that are gold-ambiguous (the hazard).
  - cost: reasoning/total tokens.

**Predictions** (from everything we've seen):
  - anchoring lifts `meaning_score` and `use_recall` **capability-independently** (flat-high across the
    ladder) — the reference-is-robust result, now for comprehensibility not binding.
  - without anchoring, comprehension is **capability-banded** (strong consumer high, weak low), and
    `confabulation_rate` **peaks at the mid tier** (the E17 hazard on the consumer side).
  - a well-lifted+anchored model + a good consumer yields high `ambiguity_flag_recall` and low `over_claim`.

**Figure**: mirror `figures_active_verification.py` — lines across the consumer ladder, one per arm, panels
per case; top row meaning_score, bottom row confabulation_rate (or ambiguity flagging). Flat-high =
capability-independent portability.

### E23 — Portability generalises across consumer TYPES (reconciliation is one)

Show the SAME anchoring that helps the asymmetric consumers (planner / auditor / operator-proxy) also
carries the reconciliation *operation*, and that each draw's without-anchoring shortfall concentrates on the
layer it stresses. Draws: the asymmetric **consumers** (single consumption) vs the reconciliation
**operation** (mutual consumption + ratification). Cross with anchoring on/off × tier. Prediction: anchoring
helps every draw capability-independently; the per-draw shortfall localises to the layer it stresses
(planner→pragmatics, auditor→provenance, reconciliation→the schematic bridge). This is the experiment that
earns "reconciliation is one draw on portability among several, and the dyadic one." Heavier (needs per-draw
tasks+golds) — Phase 2.

### E24 — The four layers as portability components (layer ablation, consumer side)

Ablate each layer from M (drop provenance, drop pragmatics, drop instances, thin the schema) and measure
comprehensibility per consumer type. Prediction: dropping a layer hurts the consumer that stresses it most;
portability = all four present. The consumer-side mirror of E19. Reuses E22 harness + the E23 consumer
types. Phase 2.

### E25 — Construct-for-portability (self-anchoring a single model)

Track A showed cognition can construct a *shared* reference between two models. New question: can cognition
construct the anchoring for a *single* model to make it portable to an arbitrary consumer (no partner)?
I.e. "self-annotate for portability" — anchor local terms to common ground, add definitions/examples/
provenance. Then measure the comprehensibility gain (feed into E22 as the CONSTRUCTED anchoring arm).
Prediction: cognition self-anchors capability-independently (it shares the common ground); the residue it
cannot add is governance-fiat authority. Reuses `construct_reference` machinery adapted to one model.
Phase 1-b (small delta on E22).

## E22 PHASE 1–2 RESULTS (complete; snapshot 2026-09-16; 54 runs = 3 cases × ladder × bare/anchored × 3 trials)

BASELINE ESTABLISHED — a solidly lifted (reference-in-lift) model is portable across essentially the WHOLE
reasoning ladder, nano included. Bare `meaning_score` (case 2, no glossary at consumption):
  cross_domain: sol 1.00 / mini 0.87 / nano 0.73  ;  big_hard: 1.00 / 0.94 / 0.89  ;  rest: 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00.
⇒ even the WEAK reasoner comprehends a good lift from the package alone — perfect on rest, ~0.9 on big_hard,
softening only to 0.73 on the hardest case (cross_domain). Portability holds nearly capability-independently;
a SOFT gradient on hard material, not a floor. The lift bakes portability in.

PHASE 2 — the consumption-time reference is a SMALL, capability-dependent top-up, only where the bare lift
fell short: cross_domain mini 0.87→1.00, nano 0.73→0.93; big_hard nano 0.89→0.94; everything else (all sol,
all of rest) unchanged. Confirms the three-case prediction: case 2 ≈ case 3 except a modest weak-tier crutch;
the reference did its real work at LIFT time.

HAZARD: small, weak-tier, hard-case-localized — only nano/cross_domain bare confabulates (0.13), and the
glossary removes it (→0). Opaque-probe honesty PASSED: big_hard `t.vendor` keeps meaning<1.0 for mini/nano
while confab≈0 (they ABSTAIN on the unknowable concept, not invent).

ANOMALY — RESOLVED (from the data, no API): the `anchored` `use_recall` drop is a METRIC ARTIFACT, not a
real effect. Recall AND precision move together (big_hard sol 1.00/0.43 → 0.67/0.29, not a conservatism
shift); absolute precision is low (0.43 bare) = the consumer over-selects because the `relevant_ids` gold is
too NARROW (a "provision end-to-end service" task legitimately touches many concepts); and the direction is
INCONSISTENT across cells (nano/rest goes 0.67→1.00, the other way) — a real effect wouldn't flip sign. ⇒
DE-WEIGHT the use-task; rely on `meaning_score` + confabulation as the portability metrics. `flag`/`over_claim`
= de-weighted Phase-3 instruments on solid models (sol over_claim=1.0 is the pilot's false alarm; ignore here).

a ladder figure (meaning_score bare-vs-anchored × case) — clone figures_active_verification.py.

## Appendix: the discussion that produced this, preserved

Kept in full because the reasoning, not just the distilled frame, is what we build on.

### Chris's argument (the setup)

1. Accepting the (A)/(B) separation ("good" is an odd word there). One concludes that (B) goods only
   support *reconciliation*, not *lift*. A reference has been identified as a (B) good.
2. Throughout the study the lift is almost always a prelude to a reconciliation, though we have now done a
   fair bit of work to separate the two.
3. This raises the question: **are references only about reconciliation?**
4. We hit the answer a while ago when we said the semantic model is **portable** — comprehensible by any
   cognitive agent or process. Reconciliation is such a cognitive process, an important one but not the
   only one.
5. So: **a successful lift generates a portable model, where portable = comprehensible to a cognitive
   agent, and such comprehensibility might need the support of references etc.** One way to consider this
   is "lift only, but applying tests for comprehensibility of the lifted result to a cognitive agent." A
   candidate basis for a new line of work.

### The response (the analysis, preserved)

- **Chris's first inference is airtight given the definitions.** (B) as defined is intrinsically
  *relational* — the shared category that authorises a binding *between two models*. Lift produces one
  model from one source; it can only yield (A). So (B) cannot be a lift good, and a reference looks like a
  reconciliation device.
- **But that conclusion is an artifact of only ever pointing the lifted model at one consumer.** The study
  has quietly assumed reconciliation is the point of a lift. Portability breaks the assumption: if the
  model is comprehensible to *any* cognitive agent, reconciliation is one consumer, and the shared category
  (B) supplies is the special case of a general thing — **the connective tissue a model needs to be
  understood by a consumer who doesn't share the author's context.** In reconciliation the consumer is
  another model and the bridge is a category both co-refer to.
- **The reframe inverts the spine.** The reference is really a *common-ground anchoring device*;
  reconciliation is what *falls out* when two models are each made portable to the *same* ground. Binding
  is a downstream consequence of mutual portability. **Portability is primary; reconciliation is the
  special case where the consumer is a peer model and the payoff is a binding.**
- **Four payoffs** (the test of "insight vs relabeling"):
  1. *Pulls (B) back into lift, under the right definition.* "(B) supports reconciliation, not lift" is true
     for narrow lift (recover own meaning = (A)) and false for extended lift (produce a *portable* model).
     Widening "lift" drags the bridging work into it, dissolving the lift/reconciliation entanglement: lift
     quality becomes measurable *directly*, not only through a downstream reconciliation.
  2. *Gives "a successful lift" the definition the study lacks* — comprehensible/usable by the intended
     consumer class — and it is testable (hand M to a non-author, non-partner consumer; measure meaning-QA,
     use, and under-anchoring flagging).
  3. *Explains what we already found.* Cognition can *construct* references (Track A) because common ground
     is shared cognition, not a formal ontology. The reference is *capability-independent* because anchoring
     meets the consumer where it already is.
  4. *Subsumes the four layers.* A usable model needs schema + meaning + provenance (who to ask) +
     pragmatics (what it's for). Portability = all four anchored to the degree the consumer needs; different
     consumers stress different layers. Reconciliation stresses the (B) bridge hardest, which is why we saw
     it first.
- **Two honest caveats.** (i) Comprehensibility is *consumer-relative* — a model portable to a strong agent
  may not be to a narrow one, so the tests run across a *consumer* ladder. (ii) Portability generalises
  *most* of (B) but not the pure **governance-fiat residue** ("which realm has the *right* to set this
  value" is authority, not comprehensibility) — that residue stays outside the frame, where a human/
  authoritative act lives. The frame has a clean edge; it doesn't eat everything.
- **New line of work = Chris's "lift only, with comprehensibility tests," made concrete.** E17 is already a
  proto-version (a strong judge grading gloss fidelity). Generalise the judge from *faithful-to-truth* to
  *usable-by-a-consumer-for-a-task*; vary the consumer across a tier ladder and across consumer *types*;
  cross with anchoring on/off. Predictions write themselves: anchoring lifts comprehensibility
  capability-independently; unanchored is banded; mid-tier consumers confabulate comprehension.
- **Net read**: run it down — it reorganises the conceptual spine rather than adding a result, and those
  are worth the most. It probably reframes the note's own thesis: "producing the inputs" is a subcase of
  "producing a portable model," and reconciliation is one reader of that model.
