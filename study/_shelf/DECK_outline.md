## SLIDE 1 — HOOK (the startling question, feet on the floor)
**[job: make a skimmer lean in]**
**HEADLINE:** *Do two capable systems still need a data model agreed in advance to work together — or can they build a good-enough one on their own, for the job at hand?*
*Body:* the question, big, centre. Under it, one quiet line of realism so the provocation reads as
**informed, not wide-eyed**: *"Decades of legacy systems that can't explain themselves, data of mixed
quality, operations that must never break — all real. The question is whether the pre-agreement, the slow
and expensive part, is still required now that the systems can reason."*
Forecast (small, bottom): *the answer we reach is yes — with real qualifications.*
`↳ reel: opens the master loop.`

## SLIDE 2 — THE BURDEN (the pain they feel)
**[job: name the audience's own ache]**
**HEADLINE:** *The standard-model quest never ends.*
*Body:* everyone must agree one vocabulary in advance — by committee, before the systems ever meet — and it
is expensive, slow, and permanently behind the world it models. Capable engineers, working independently,
build different but defensible models of the same network; someone always has to reconcile them.
*One line:* *"What if the systems could do that reconciling themselves?"*
`↳ reel: closes "why is this hard today", opens "what could lift the burden".`

## SLIDE 3 — THE REFRAME (cognition changes the premise)
**[job: introduce the one idea the whole deck rests on]**
**HEADLINE:** *A model that can stand on its own.*
*Body:* the shift — once each side can **reason**, a system's data can be **lifted** into a self-describing
form that any capable agent can pick up and use **from the package alone, with nothing agreed in advance.**
Plain-words definition on slide: *"Portable = a sufficiently reasoning agent can make full use of it without
asking anyone for clarification outside the package."*
`↳ reel: closes "what could lift the burden", opens "does that actually work?".`

## SLIDE 4 — THE PATH (transition is a first-class character)
**[job: promise a mapped path, not a fantasy]**
**HEADLINE:** *How do we get there, tractably — and safely?*
*Body:* frame the work as **mapping the path**, not flipping a switch: inert legacy and cognitive systems
coexisting; mixed-quality data handled gracefully; a light shared **reference/anchor** as the tool that makes
each step safe. Two questions to carry forward: *can a lifted model really be understood cold?* and *when two
models meet, can cognition bridge them without a pre-agreed standard?*
`↳ reel: opens Part 1.`

### PART 1 — PORTABILITY (three lures)

## SLIDE 5 — LURE P1 (the baseline proof)
**[job: prove the core claim in one breath]**
**HEADLINE:** *A well-lifted model is understood cold — by strong, middle, and even weak readers.*
*Body:* hand a lifted model to an independent agent that never saw the system; ask it to explain each concept
from the package alone. **Near-perfect comprehension across the board**, softening only for the weakest reader
on the hardest material (strong 1.00 / mid 0.87 / weak 0.73 on the toughest cross-domain seam; perfect on
routine material). Holds across **six independent cases** (bare 0.98, anchored 1.00) and **across model
families** (a strong reader from a different family reads it as well as the home one).
*Feet-on-floor:* the weak tier can confabulate — there is an **honest floor**, not magic.
`↳ reel: closes "can it be understood cold?" (yes), opens "what if the source is too thin to understand?".`

## SLIDE 6 — LURE P2 (cognition builds the missing ground) — the money slide
**[job: the direct answer to the burden]**
**HEADLINE:** *When the ground isn't there, cognition builds it.*
*Body:* two private models, no shared standard, nothing recognisable. Left alone, a careful agent refuses to
guess and binds almost nothing (**0.20**). Let the two agents **construct a thin shared reference themselves**
and then bind through it: they close to **0.80**, and to **0.90** when they run a quick decisive check —
approaching the **1.00** you'd get from a ready-made standard. *They don't need a standard handed to them;
they can make one.*
`↳ reel: closes "what if the source is too thin?" (build the ground), opens "is a shared token enough?".`

## SLIDE 7 — LURE P3 (meaning, not tokens — the credibility catch)
**[job: show the discipline / the honest edge]**
**HEADLINE:** *A name with nothing behind it is a trap.*
*Body:* portability is about **meaning-bearing content, not surface**. A lift from a surface that keeps
concept names but strips their meaning is **no safer than an anonymous one** — a bare name invites a
plausible, wrong reading. Same law on the reference side: a shared pointer with **no description is worse than
nothing** (drops below the no-reference floor). What makes a lift portable is meaning; a reference used **in
the lift** repairs a thin one.
`↳ reel: closes Part 1 — portability is real, and we know exactly what breaks it — opens the turn.`

## SLIDE 8 — THE RECONCILIATION TURN
**[job: pivot to the use case that matters]**
**HEADLINE:** *The real prize: two systems that never agreed on anything, working together.*
*Body:* portability is the object; **reconciliation is the flagship use** — aligning two independently built
models so the systems interoperate. It is **its own job**, not a patch on a bad lift: two perfectly good
models still diverge (one says *circuit*, the other *bearer*; a *grade* that looks shared but isn't), and
bridging that divergence is the work.
`↳ reel: opens Part 2.`

## SLIDE 9 — LURE R1 (bind without a standard — and REFUSE the wrong ones) — the clincher
**[job: the credibility clincher]**
**HEADLINE:** *They bind what matches — and refuse what only looks like it — with no standard, at the cost of a short glossary.*
*Body:* two agents reconcile two independently authored models correctly and **ad hoc**. The discipline is the
point: a planted look-alike — a transport *grade* vs an IP *grade*, same word, unrelated meaning — is
**refused**, with or without help. Where they need a nudge, the fix is a **thin shared reference (a short
glossary), not a universal standard** — and reconciling through one shared anchor turns work that grew
**quadratically** into work that grows **linearly** as systems are added.
*Feet-on-floor:* this is disciplined refusal, not naïve agreement.
`↳ reel: closes "can they bridge without a standard?" (yes, and safely), opens "where does the machine stop?".`

## SLIDE 10 — LURE R2 (the automatable end, and the honest guardrail)
**[job: land the payoff AND the limit together]**
**HEADLINE:** *Where both sides can reason, it finishes on its own — and it knows where to stop.*
*Body:* at the fully-cognitive end, reconciliation completes **autonomously** — negotiations and
significance judgements included, no human in the loop. As cognition recedes, it **degrades honestly**: it
defers rather than guesses, and a thin reference buys back the facts an inert side can't supply. **The
honest guardrail (keep visible):** a reference supplies *information*, never *authority* — the one decision
that stays with a human is **whose value governs when two sides genuinely conflict.**
`↳ reel: closes "where does the machine stop?" — cleanly, at authority — opens the recap.`

## SLIDE 11 — RECAP (the promise held)
**[job: one-glance proof the opening question was answered]**
**HEADLINE:** *Yes — with real qualifications.*
*Body:* three lines: **portability is real** (understood cold, across readers and families); **cognition
bridges divergence** (builds the ground, refuses the traps, no standard needed); **it automates where both
sides reason, and hands off cleanly where authority — not meaning — is at stake.** Every limit shown is a
credibility signal, not a hedge.
`↳ reel: closes the master loop.`

## SLIDE 12 — TRANSFORMATIVE CLOSE (redirection, not elimination)
**[job: make it feel big and actionable, invite the full work]**
**HEADLINE:** *This changes what we should standardise — and lightens the load.*
*Body:* not "standards are over." **Production and agreement can increasingly be ad hoc**, machine-to-machine,
for the task at hand — coexisting with legacy, redirecting the standards effort toward the anchors and
authority boundaries that actually need agreeing. A roadmap, not a rupture.
*CTA:* the full evidence — six cases, four scenarios, cross-family checks — is in the report. *Come see it.*
`↳ reel: converts.`

## SLIDE 13 — (optional interstitial / backup) THE MAP
**[job: for a reader who wants one glance of rigor before committing]**
**HEADLINE:** *Four scenarios, chosen to traverse the space — not four anecdotes.*
*Body:* one small 2-axis picture: identity↔relation × where the divergence sits (schematic / instance /
provenance / pragmatic / abstraction), with the four scenarios (configuration, intent, cross-domain,
observability) placed as a deliberate traverse. Use only if the room wants proof-of-breadth before the CTA;
otherwise keep as backup.
