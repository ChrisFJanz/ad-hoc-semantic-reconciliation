# Report narrative brief — bringing the deck's hooks into the master report (governing doc)

Working brief for the master report's rewrite. Sits BESIDE `deck_brief.md`. The deck and the report share
one spine and one tone; they differ in economy. This note says how the report borrows the deck's interest
mechanics WITHOUT borrowing its selectivity. Execute the rewrite against this, not against the old
apparatus-catalogue structure.

## The core idea: same spine, inverted economy, two reading depths

The report has been paying a hidden penalty for reading like a catalogue of apparatus. The deck work already
solved the narrative problem; the report should lift the deck's **spine** while **inverting** its economy.

- **Spine transfers whole.** Same startling question to open (*do two sufficiently cognitive systems need
  pre-agreed data models at all, or can they make one on their own?*); same burden -> reframe -> path setup;
  same portability-first ordering (Part 1 lift/portability, Part 2 reconciliation-as-use); same governing
  tone (feet-on-floor AND audacious) holding at every point. Structure and voice both transfer.
- **Economy inverts.** In the deck the question cadence LICENSES selectivity — one finding per question,
  kill the darlings, because the deck's job is desire, not completeness. In the report the SAME cadence does
  the opposite work: each question is the **onramp to a full section** that then plays out the evidence, the
  caveats, the negative controls, the methods. The hooks don't replace rigor; they usher the reader INTO it.
- **Two reading depths in one document** (the payoff). A skimmer follows the questions and the boxed headline
  findings (the deck's "lures," rendered as pull-quotes / callout boxes) and gets the story. A committed
  reader reads through into the full evidence beneath each. Story on top, spec underneath, same page. You do
  NOT choose between narrative and apparatus.

## Section mechanic (the reel as connective tissue)

Each major section: **poses its question as the heading -> opens with a one-line stake (why it matters) ->
delivers the whole apparatus (design, results, caveats, negative controls, methods) -> closes by opening the
next question.** The question->finding->bigger-question cadence becomes momentum BETWEEN sections, never a
substitute for depth WITHIN them. One clear question per section — not a hook on every subsection (over-
hooking reads as breathless).

## Two traps (each the mirror of a deck freedom)

1. **Do not let hooks soften or crowd the negative results.** In the report the degradation control, the
   weak-tier confabulation, and the pure-authority/governance residue ARE the credibility — a deck slide may
   legitimately gesture at them; the report must deliver them in full. The narrative framing should lead the
   reader toward these with appetite, never paper over them.
2. **Do not sacrifice navigability.** A report is also consulted non-linearly, so the arc must coexist with
   findable headings, tables, and reference-ability. The story is a LAYER over a well-structured document,
   not a replacement for structure.

## Tone calibration (vs the deck)

Hold the same governing tone from `deck_brief.md` (audacious in the questions/possibility; measured in the
evidence; actionable in the implication; evolutionary not revolutionary; redirection not elimination; the
five naive traps to never trip). But calibrate the STARTLE down a notch: the deck converts someone who has
not committed, so it provokes; the report's reader is already in the boat, so the same hooks should read as
**orient and motivate**, not provoke. The report EARNS the audacity harder because it shows all the work.

## Structural choice (decided)

**Woven, not bolted-on.** Framing-at-the-head-of-each-Part (narrative interleaved section by section), NOT a
single deck-like executive narrative up front followed by a dry body. Readers see through a "fun intro"
stapled to an apparatus dump; interleaving keeps story and evidence woven throughout.
